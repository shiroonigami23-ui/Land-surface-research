**Land degradation** is the long-term loss of the land's ability to produce food, store water and support life. When it happens in drylands it is called **desertification**. It does not mean deserts marching forward like sand waves; it is patchy decline driven mostly by how people use land, made worse by drought.

:::facts The scale of the problem
- Up to **40% of the world's land** is degraded, and droughts have grown in frequency and duration by **29% since 2000** (UN, at UNCCD COP17, Mongolia, August 2026)
- Land degradation and drought cost about **$878 billion a year**; Sub-Saharan Africa holds **45%** of the world's degraded land
- About **3.2 billion people** are affected (IPBES 2018)
- India: about **97.85 million hectares** (~30% of its area) was undergoing degradation in 2018–19 (ISRO's Desertification and Land Degradation Atlas, 2021)
:::

## Causes

:::collapse !What degrades land
- **Water erosion:** rain washes topsoil off bare or ploughed slopes. The main process in India.
- **Wind erosion:** dry, bare soil blows away (the Dust Bowl, the Thar margins).
- **Salinisation and waterlogging:** irrigation without drainage raises salty groundwater (Punjab, Haryana, Indus basin, Aral Sea basin).
- **Nutrient mining and loss of organic matter:** taking crops off year after year without returning residues or manure.
- **Overgrazing:** too many animals strip grasslands bare.
- **Deforestation:** removes roots that hold soil and the litter that feeds it.
- **Compaction** by heavy machines; **sealing** by roads and buildings; **mining**.
- **Pollution:** heavy metals, plastics and excess agrochemicals.
:::

## UNCCD and COP17

The **UN Convention to Combat Desertification (UNCCD)**, adopted in 1994, is one of the three "Rio conventions" (with climate and biodiversity). Its **17th Conference of the Parties (COP17)** concluded in **Ulaanbaatar, Mongolia, in August 2026**, focusing on rangelands, drought resilience and finance for land restoration. Countries have pledged to restore about **1 billion hectares** by 2030; India pledged **26 million hectares**.

## Restoration that works

- **Loess Plateau, China:** terracing, banning grazing on slopes and replanting turned one of the world's most eroded landscapes green, raising incomes for about 2.5 million people.
- **Farmer-managed natural regeneration (FMNR), Niger:** farmers protected tree stumps sprouting in their fields, regreening around 5 million hectares.
- **Zaï pits and stone bunds (Burkina Faso):** small planting pits filled with manure catch rainwater and revive crusted soils.
- **Great Green Wall:** a mosaic of restoration across the Sahel, from Senegal to Djibouti.
- **India:** watershed villages such as **Ralegan Siddhi** and **Hiware Bazar** (Maharashtra) recharged groundwater with check dams and grazing rules; reclamation of *usar* land in UP with gypsum; Rajasthan's *johad* revival led by Tarun Bharat Sangh revived the Arvari river.
