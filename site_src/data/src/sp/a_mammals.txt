# Big cats & wild cats|🐅
Bengal tiger|Panthera tigris tigris|India; Bangladesh; Nepal; Bhutan|Tropical and dry forests, mangroves, grasslands|Deer, wild boar, gaur|EN|India holds about 75% of the world's wild tigers (3,682 in the 2022 census), recovering under Project Tiger since 1973.|A deep roar audible up to 3 km away, plus chuffing (a friendly puffing greeting), growls and moans.
Siberian tiger|Panthera tigris altaica|Russia; China|Temperate and boreal forest|Deer, wild boar|EN|The largest tiger subspecies, adapted to −40 °C winters in the Russian Far East.|Powerful roars and low growls; chuffs to greet.
Sumatran tiger|Panthera tigris sondaica|Indonesia|Lowland and montane rainforest|Deer, wild pigs|CR|The smallest surviving tiger, with fewer than ~600 left on Sumatra.
Asiatic lion|Panthera leo leo|India|Dry deciduous forest and scrub|Deer, antelope, livestock|EN|Survives only in and around Gujarat's Gir Forest, numbering about 891 in 2025.|Deep, rolling roars at dusk carry up to 8 km.
African lion|Panthera leo|Africa|Savanna, grassland|Zebra, wildebeest, buffalo|VU|The only truly social cat, living in prides led by related females.|The loudest roar of any cat, heard 8 km away, used to mark territory.
Leopard|Panthera pardus|Africa; Asia|Forest, savanna, mountains, edges of cities|Deer, monkeys, dogs|VU|The most adaptable big cat; lives even inside Mumbai's Sanjay Gandhi National Park.|A rasping 'sawing' call like wood being sawn, repeated rhythmically.
Snow leopard|Panthera uncia|India; Nepal; China; Mongolia; Central Asia|Alpine and subalpine mountains 3,000–5,500 m|Blue sheep, ibex, marmots|VU|The 'ghost of the mountains', with a thick tail used for balance and warmth.|Cannot roar; makes a soft puffing 'chuff', mews, hisses and yowls.
Jaguar|Panthera onca|Mexico; Brazil; Argentina|Rainforest, wetlands (Pantanal)|Capybara, caimans, peccaries|NT|Has the strongest bite of any big cat for its size and kills by piercing the skull.|A hoarse, coughing grunt repeated several times.
Cheetah|Acinonyx jubatus|Africa; Iran; India (reintroduced)|Savanna, open grassland|Gazelles, impala|VU|The fastest land animal (~100–110 km/h); reintroduced to India's Kuno National Park in 2022.|Cannot roar; chirps like a bird, purrs and makes a stuttering 'churr'.
Clouded leopard|Neofelis nebulosa|Northeast India; Southeast Asia|Tropical forest|Monkeys, birds, deer|VU|Has the longest canine teeth for its size of any living cat.
Eurasian lynx|Lynx lynx|Europe; Russia; Central Asia; Ladakh|Forest, mountains|Roe deer, hares|LC|The largest lynx, with black ear tufts.
Caracal|Caracal caracal|Africa; Middle East; India|Dry scrub, semi-desert|Birds, rodents, hares|LC|Can leap 3 m to swat birds out of the air.
Fishing cat|Prionailurus viverrinus|India; Bangladesh; Southeast Asia|Wetlands, mangroves|Fish, frogs|VU|The state animal of West Bengal; dives for fish with partly webbed feet.
Jungle cat|Felis chaus|Asia; Egypt|Reedbeds, scrub, farmland|Rodents, birds|LC|India's most common wild cat.
Pallas's cat|Otocolobus manul|Mongolia; China; Ladakh; Central Asia|Cold steppe, rocky slopes|Pikas, voles|LC|A fluffy, flat-faced cat of the cold steppe with round pupils.
Puma|Puma concolor|Americas|Mountains, forest, desert|Deer|LC|Has more common names than any animal (cougar, mountain lion, panther).|Cannot roar; makes eerie screams, chirps and whistles.
Rusty-spotted cat|Prionailurus rubiginosus|India; Sri Lanka; Nepal|Dry forest, grassland|Rodents, birds|NT|One of the smallest wild cats, weighing about 1–1.6 kg.
Serval|Leptailurus serval|Africa|Savanna wetlands|Rodents, birds|LC|Has the longest legs relative to body of any cat.
Ocelot|Leopardus pardalis|Americas|Rainforest|Rodents, birds|LC|A nocturnal spotted cat of the American tropics.
# Elephants, rhinos & large herbivores|🐘
Asian elephant|Elephas maximus|India; Sri Lanka; Southeast Asia|Forest, grassland|Grass, bark, fruit|EN|India has about 27,000 wild elephants, roughly 60% of the species; India's national heritage animal.|Trumpets, squeaks, roars and deep rumbles, some infrasonic and felt more than heard.
African bush elephant|Loxodonta africana|Sub-Saharan Africa|Savanna, woodland|Grass, leaves, bark|EN|The largest land animal, up to ~6 tonnes; calls each other by name-like rumbles.|Low rumbles that travel kilometres through the ground, trumpets and roars.
African forest elephant|Loxodonta cyclotis|Congo Basin; West Africa|Rainforest|Fruit, leaves|CR|A smaller, separate species that spreads the seeds of many rainforest trees.
Indian rhinoceros|Rhinoceros unicornis|India; Nepal|Floodplain grassland|Grass, reeds|VU|Recovered from ~200 in 1900 to over 4,000, two-thirds in Assam's Kaziranga.|Snorts, honks, bleats and moos.
Javan rhinoceros|Rhinoceros sondaicus|Indonesia|Lowland rainforest|Leaves, shoots|CR|Survives only in Ujung Kulon National Park, about 76 animals.
Sumatran rhinoceros|Dicerorhinus sumatrensis|Indonesia|Rainforest|Leaves, twigs, fruit|CR|The hairy, smallest rhino; fewer than 50 remain.|Whale-like whistles, 'eeps' and humming calls.
White rhinoceros|Ceratotherium simum|Southern Africa|Grassland|Grass|NT|A grazer with a wide square lip; the northern subspecies has only two females left.
Black rhinoceros|Diceros bicornis|Eastern and southern Africa|Bush, savanna|Shrubs, branches|CR|A browser with a hooked upper lip; numbers recovering from poaching.
Hippopotamus|Hippopotamus amphibius|Sub-Saharan Africa|Rivers, lakes|Grass|VU|Spends days in water and grazes at night; one of Africa's most dangerous animals.|Loud 'wheeze honks' heard over a kilometre, partly under water.
Giraffe|Giraffa camelopardalis|Africa|Savanna|Acacia leaves|VU|The tallest animal (to 5.5 m); some populations have fallen sharply.|Mostly quiet; low humming at night, snorts and grunts.
Gaur|Bos gaurus|India; Southeast Asia|Forest, hills|Grass, leaves|VU|The largest wild cattle, called the 'Indian bison'.|Deep bellows and whistling snorts.
Wild water buffalo|Bubalus arnee|India; Nepal|Wet grassland, swamps|Grass, aquatic plants|EN|Ancestor of the domestic water buffalo, mainly in Kaziranga.
American bison|Bison bison|USA; Canada|Prairie|Grass|NT|Fell from ~30–60 million to under 1,000 by 1890; now ~500,000.|Bulls roar and bellow during the rut.
European bison|Bison bonasus|Poland; Belarus; Europe|Forest|Grass, bark|NT|Saved from extinction in zoos and reintroduced to Białowieża Forest.
Yak|Bos mutus|Tibet; Ladakh|High plateau grassland|Grass, sedges|VU|Wild yaks live above 4,000 m; domestic yaks carry loads across the Himalaya.|Grunts (its name Bos grunniens means 'grunting ox').
# Primates|🐒
Bengal slow loris|Nycticebus bengalensis|Northeast India; Southeast Asia|Tropical forest|Insects, gum, nectar|EN|A slow, nocturnal primate with a venomous bite.
Hoolock gibbon|Hoolock hoolock|Northeast India; Bangladesh; Myanmar|Evergreen forest|Fruit, leaves|EN|India's only ape.|Loud, haunting duets 'hoo-lock' sung by pairs at dawn.
Lion-tailed macaque|Macaca silenus|India (Western Ghats)|Evergreen rainforest|Fruit, insects|EN|Has a silver mane and lion-like tail; only ~4,000 remain.
Rhesus macaque|Macaca mulatta|India; South and East Asia|Forest, cities, temples|Fruit, seeds, food scraps|LC|Used in research that developed the polio vaccine and the Rh blood factor.
Bonnet macaque|Macaca radiata|South India|Forest, towns|Fruit, seeds|VU|Named for its cap-like whorl of hair.
Northern plains gray langur|Semnopithecus entellus|India|Forest, towns|Leaves, fruit|LC|The 'Hanuman langur', sacred in Hinduism.|Loud 'whoops' and barking alarm calls warn of tigers and leopards.
Nilgiri langur|Semnopithecus johnii|India (Western Ghats)|Shola forest|Leaves|VU|A black langur with a golden-brown head.
Golden langur|Trachypithecus geei|India (Assam); Bhutan|Forest|Leaves, fruit|EN|A golden-coated monkey of the Manas region.
Bornean orangutan|Pongo pygmaeus|Borneo|Rainforest|Fruit|CR|Builds a new nest every night and uses tools.|Males give 'long calls' of booming roars that last up to 4 minutes.
Sumatran orangutan|Pongo abelii|Indonesia|Rainforest|Fruit, insects|CR|More social than its Bornean cousin.
Western gorilla|Gorilla gorilla|Central Africa|Rainforest|Leaves, fruit|CR|The largest primate, living in family groups led by a silverback.|Hoots, grunts, belches and chest-beating that carries 1 km.
Mountain gorilla|Gorilla beringei beringei|Rwanda; Uganda; DR Congo|Montane forest|Leaves, stems|EN|Recovered to over 1,000 through gorilla tourism and protection.
Chimpanzee|Pan troglodytes|Central and West Africa|Forest, savanna|Fruit, insects, meat|EN|Shares about 98.8% of our DNA; uses tools such as termite-fishing sticks.|Pant-hoots that build to screams, drumming on tree roots.
Bonobo|Pan paniscus|DR Congo|Rainforest|Fruit|EN|A peaceful, female-led ape.
Ring-tailed lemur|Lemur catta|Madagascar|Dry forest|Fruit, leaves|EN|Sunbathes in groups and has 'stink fights' using scented tails.
Aye-aye|Daubentonia madagascariensis|Madagascar|Rainforest|Grubs, nuts|EN|Taps on wood and hooks grubs out with a thin middle finger.
Mantled howler|Alouatta palliata|Central and South America|Rainforest|Leaves|VU|Among the loudest land animals.|Roars audible 5 km away, made with an enlarged hyoid bone.
Golden snub-nosed monkey|Rhinopithecus roxellana|China|Mountain forest|Lichens, leaves|EN|Lives in snowy mountain forests.
Japanese macaque|Macaca fuscata|Japan|Forest, snowy mountains|Fruit, bark|LC|The 'snow monkey' that bathes in hot springs.
Proboscis monkey|Nasalis larvatus|Borneo|Mangroves, riverine forest|Leaves|EN|Males have huge noses that amplify their calls.
# Bears, dogs & other carnivores|🐻
Sloth bear|Melursus ursinus|India; Sri Lanka; Nepal|Dry forest, scrub|Termites, ants, fruit|VU|Sucks up termites through a gap in its front teeth.|Loud huffing, sucking and snorting while feeding; roars when threatened.
Asian black bear|Ursus thibetanus|Himalaya; East Asia|Mountain forest|Fruit, nuts, insects|VU|Has a white V on its chest.
Himalayan brown bear|Ursus arctos isabellinus|India; Pakistan; Nepal|Alpine meadows|Grass, roots, marmots|LC|A rare, sandy-coloured brown bear of the high Himalaya.
Grizzly bear|Ursus arctos horribilis|North America|Forest, mountains|Salmon, berries, roots|LC|North America's brown bear, famous for fishing salmon.
Polar bear|Ursus maritimus|Arctic|Sea ice, coasts|Seals|VU|Hunts seals from sea ice that is shrinking with warming.
Giant panda|Ailuropoda melanoleuca|China|Bamboo forest|Bamboo|VU|Eats 12–38 kg of bamboo a day; downlisted from Endangered in 2016.|Bleats like a goat, honks, barks and chirps.
Red panda|Ailurus fulgens|Sikkim; Arunachal; Nepal; Bhutan; China|Bamboo forest|Bamboo, fruit|EN|The state animal of Sikkim; not closely related to the giant panda.
Sun bear|Helarctos malayanus|Southeast Asia; Northeast India|Rainforest|Insects, honey, fruit|VU|The smallest bear, with a very long tongue for honey.
Dhole|Cuon alpinus|India; Central and Southeast Asia|Forest|Deer, wild pigs|EN|The 'whistling dog' hunts in packs; India holds most of the world's dholes.|Whistles to keep the pack together, plus screams and chatters.
Indian wolf|Canis lupus pallipes|India; Middle East|Grassland, scrub|Antelope, livestock|LC|One of the oldest wolf lineages; under 3,000 remain in India.|Howls, rarely heard as Indian wolves howl less than northern wolves.
Gray wolf|Canis lupus|North America; Eurasia|Forest, tundra|Elk, deer|LC|Ancestor of the dog; reintroduction to Yellowstone in 1995 reshaped the ecosystem.|Long howls that carry 10 km, plus barks, whimpers and growls.
Ethiopian wolf|Canis simensis|Ethiopia|Afroalpine grassland|Mole-rats|EN|Africa's most endangered carnivore, fewer than 500 left.
Golden jackal|Canis aureus|India; Middle East; SE Europe|Scrub, farmland|Rodents, carrion, fruit|LC|Common around Indian villages.|Eerie group howls and yips at dusk.
Indian fox|Vulpes bengalensis|India; Nepal; Pakistan|Grassland, scrub|Rodents, insects|LC|A small fox with a black-tipped tail.
Red fox|Vulpes vulpes|Northern Hemisphere; Australia|Almost everywhere incl. cities|Rodents, fruit|LC|The most widespread wild carnivore.|Screams, barks and gekkering chatter.
Arctic fox|Vulpes lagopus|Arctic|Tundra|Lemmings, carrion|LC|Turns white in winter.
Fennec fox|Vulpes zerda|Sahara|Sand desert|Insects, rodents|LC|Huge ears radiate heat and hear prey underground.
African wild dog|Lycaon pictus|Sub-Saharan Africa|Savanna|Antelope|EN|Pack hunters with ~80% success, among the highest of any predator.|Twittering calls and 'hoo' contact calls; they 'vote' by sneezing.
Striped hyena|Hyaena hyaena|India; Middle East; Africa|Dry scrub|Carrion|NT|A shy scavenger of Indian drylands.
Spotted hyena|Crocuta crocuta|Sub-Saharan Africa|Savanna|Antelope, carrion|LC|A powerful hunter, not just a scavenger.|The famous 'laugh' (giggle) and long 'whoops' heard at night.
Honey badger|Mellivora capensis|Africa; India; Middle East|Scrub, forest|Rodents, snakes, honey|LC|Famously fearless; raids beehives.
Smooth-coated otter|Lutrogale perspicillata|India; South Asia|Rivers, wetlands|Fish|VU|Hunts fish in family groups.
Eurasian otter|Lutra lutra|Europe; Asia|Rivers|Fish|NT|Returning to British rivers after pollution cleanup.
Asian palm civet|Paradoxurus hermaphroditus|India; Southeast Asia|Forest, towns|Fruit|LC|Its droppings make the costly kopi luwak coffee.
Indian grey mongoose|Urva edwardsii|India; Pakistan; Iran|Scrub, farmland|Rodents, snakes|LC|Rikki-Tikki-Tavi; fights cobras.
Meerkat|Suricata suricatta|Southern Africa|Kalahari|Insects|LC|Takes turns as sentry.|Different alarm calls for eagles and jackals.
Wolverine|Gulo gulo|Arctic, boreal forest|Tundra, taiga|Carrion, small mammals|LC|The largest land weasel, famous for strength.
Raccoon|Procyon lotor|North America; Europe (introduced)|Forest, cities|Anything|LC|Dexterous, clever urban forager.
