🟤 Soils

Every major soil type: the 12 USDA soil orders, the soils of India, and famous soils such as chernozem, loess and terra preta, with fertility and the crops they support.

Soil orders (USDA)

Mollisols

Deep, dark grassland soils rich in organic matter; the world's breadbasket soils.

Soil orders (USDA)

Alfisols

Moderately weathered forest soils with good base saturation.

Soil orders (USDA)

Ultisols

Strongly weathered, acidic soils of warm humid regions.

Soil orders (USDA)

Oxisols

The most weathered soils, rich in iron and aluminium oxides; nutrients sit in the forest, not the soil.

Soil orders (USDA)

Aridisols

Dry soils that often hold salt or lime layers.

Soil orders (USDA)

Vertisols

Clay soils that swell when wet and crack when dry; India's black cotton soil.

Soil orders (USDA)

Entisols

Young soils with little development, e.g. fresh river alluvium.

Soil orders (USDA)

Inceptisols

Young soils beginning to develop horizons.

Soil orders (USDA)

Andisols

Fertile soils formed from volcanic ash.

Soil orders (USDA)

Spodosols

Acidic forest soils with a bleached layer above iron-rich subsoil.

Soil orders (USDA)

Histosols

Organic soils storing about a third of soil carbon on 3% of land.

Soil orders (USDA)

Gelisols

Soils with permafrost.

Soils of India

Alluvial soil

India's most widespread and productive soil, deposited by rivers (khadar new, bhangar old).

Soils of India

Black cotton soil (regur)

Formed from Deccan basalt; holds moisture and self-ploughs by cracking.

Soils of India

Red soil

Red from iron oxide, formed on ancient crystalline rocks.

Soils of India

Laterite soil

Leached by heavy rain; hardens into building blocks when exposed.

Soils of India

Arid (desert) soil

Sandy and saline; productive when irrigated.

Soils of India

Saline and alkaline soil (usar)

Salt build-up, often from over-irrigation; reclaimed with gypsum.

Soils of India

Peaty and marshy soil

Waterlogged soils rich in organic matter.

Soils of India

Forest and mountain soil

Thin, organic-rich soils on slopes.

Other important soils

Chernozem

'Black earth' up to a metre thick, among the most fertile soils on Earth.

Other important soils

Loess

Wind-blown silt that forms fertile soils but erodes easily.

Other important soils

Terra preta

Fertile 'dark earth' created by Indigenous Amazonians with charcoal and waste over centuries.

Other important soils

Podzol

European name for spodosols.

Other important soils

Calcisol

Soils with accumulated calcium carbonate.

Other important soils

Gleysol

Grey-blue soils saturated by groundwater.

Other important soils

Nitisol

Among the most productive tropical soils.
