**Soil** is the thin, loose layer at Earth's surface where rock, air, water and life meet. A typical healthy soil is about **45% minerals, 25% water, 25% air and 5% organic matter** by volume. One teaspoon can hold more microorganisms than there are people on Earth. Soil grows about **95% of our food**, stores more carbon than the atmosphere and all plants combined, and filters most of our drinking water.

## How soil forms: five factors

Soil scientist **Vasily Dokuchaev** (Russia, 1880s) and later **Hans Jenny** (1941) described soil as the product of five factors, remembered as **CLORPT**:
1. **Climate:** rain and temperature control weathering and leaching. Hot, wet climates make deep red, leached soils; dry climates make thin, salty soils.
2. **Organisms:** plants, roots, fungi, bacteria, earthworms and termites mix and enrich soil.
3. **Relief (topography):** slopes lose soil downhill; valleys collect it.
4. **Parent material:** the rock or sediment underneath (basalt gives black soils; granite gives sandy red soils; river silt gives alluvium).
5. **Time:** forming just **2.5 cm (1 inch) of topsoil can take several hundred years**.

## Soil layers (horizons)

| Horizon | Name | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| O | Organic | Leaf litter and humus |
| A | Topsoil | Dark, mixed with organic matter, full of roots and life |
| E | Eluviated | Leached, pale layer (common in forest soils) |
| B | Subsoil | Collects clay, iron and minerals washed down from above |
| C | Parent material | Weathered rock |
| R | Bedrock | Solid rock |

## Texture: sand, silt and clay

Particles are sorted by size: **sand** (2–0.05 mm, gritty, drains fast), **silt** (0.05–0.002 mm, smooth) and **clay** (under 0.002 mm, sticky, holds water and nutrients). A balanced mix is **loam**, the farmer's ideal. Soil **pH** matters too: most crops prefer 6–7.5; tea and blueberries like acid soils; alkaline *usar* soils need gypsum.

## The world's soil orders

The USDA Soil Taxonomy recognises **12 soil orders**. The richest for farming are **mollisols** (grassland "black earths") and **andisols** (volcanic ash). The most weathered and nutrient-poor are **oxisols** in the tropics. Browse them all in the [soil catalog](/wiki/soils/index.html).

:::facts Soil in numbers
- About a **third of the world's soils** are moderately to highly degraded (FAO)
- Global soil erosion by water: ~36 billion tonnes a year (Borrelli et al. 2017)
- Soils hold about **1,500–2,400 billion tonnes** of organic carbon in the top metre or two
- World Soil Day: **5 December**
:::
