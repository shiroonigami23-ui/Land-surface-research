Rainfall Patterns and the Monsoon

Why rain falls where it does, the world's wettest and driest places, how the Indian monsoon works, and how the 2026 monsoon ended 13% below normal.

📅 Reviewed 2026-11-15⏱ 3 min read🔗 2 sources🎓 Intermediate

Rain forms when moist air rises, cools and condenses. Air is lifted in three ways, and each makes a distinct rainfall pattern:

  1. Convectional rain: the Sun heats the ground, warm air shoots upward and builds afternoon thunderstorms. Typical of the equator and of Indian pre-monsoon storms (kalbaisakhi in Bengal, mango showers in Kerala).
  2. Orographic (relief) rain: wind is forced up a mountain. The windward side is soaked and the leeward side lies in a rain shadow, as along the Western Ghats.
  3. Frontal (cyclonic) rain: warm air rides over cold air along a front, giving the steady rain of Europe and North America, and India’s winter western disturbances, which bring snow to the Himalaya and rain for the wheat crop.

The Indian monsoon#

Monsoon comes from the Arabic mausim, “season”. In summer, the land of South Asia heats far faster than the Indian Ocean. Low pressure deepens over the northwest of the subcontinent, the ITCZ moves north to about 20–25°N, and moist southwesterly winds blow in from the ocean. The Tibetan Plateau, heated in summer, strengthens the circulation, and the Himalaya trap the moisture.

How the season unfolds
- **Onset:** the monsoon reaches Kerala around **1 June** and covers all India by about **8 July**. - **Two branches:** the *Arabian Sea branch* hits the Western Ghats; the *Bay of Bengal branch* swings up into the Northeast (Mawsynram, Cherrapunji) and then west along the Himalaya. - **Breaks:** dry spells of a week or more when the monsoon trough moves to the Himalayan foothills. - **Withdrawal:** from Rajasthan starting around 17 September, from the whole country by mid-October. - **Northeast (winter) monsoon:** October–December, bringing most of the year's rain to coastal Tamil Nadu.

About 70–80% of India’s annual rainfall falls in June–September. More than half of India’s cropland is rain-fed, so a good monsoon boosts the kharif harvest (rice, cotton, soybeans, pulses) and refills reservoirs and groundwater for the rabi (winter) crop.

What makes a monsoon good or bad#

  • El Niño (warm eastern Pacific) tends to weaken the Indian monsoon; most major Indian droughts (1877, 1918, 1972, 1987, 2002, 2009) were El Niño years. La Niña tends to strengthen it.
  • Indian Ocean Dipole (IOD): a warmer western Indian Ocean (positive IOD) can boost rainfall even in El Niño years, as in 1997.
  • Snow cover over Eurasia and the Madden–Julian Oscillation also play a part.

The 2026 monsoon#

The India Meteorological Department reported that June–September 2026 rainfall was 87% of the long-period average: 759.4 mm against a normal of 868.6 mm, a “deficient” season. The shortfall was steepest in East and Northeast India (74%) and the South Peninsula (76%).

Changing rainfall#

Total monsoon rainfall over India has not changed much in the long run, but its character has: fewer moderate rain days, more dry spells and more extreme downpours (over 150 mm a day), which cause floods such as Kerala 2018 and Wayanad 2024 and are hard for soils to absorb.
