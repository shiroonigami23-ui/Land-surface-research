Droughts: Kinds, Causes and History

Meteorological, agricultural, hydrological and socio-economic drought; what causes them; the worst droughts and famines in history; and how droughts are getting longer.

📅 Reviewed 2026-11-15⏱ 2 min read🔗 3 sources🎓 Intermediate

A drought is a long period of unusually low water supply. Unlike a flood or cyclone it creeps in slowly, has no clear start or end, and can cover whole continents. Droughts have become more frequent and longer by about 29% since 2000, according to the UN, and together with land degradation they cost the world an estimated $878 billion a year.

Four kinds of drought#

From missing rain to empty plates
1. **Meteorological drought:** rainfall well below normal for a region. India's IMD calls a season "deficient" when all-India rain is below 90% of normal. 2. **Agricultural drought:** soil moisture too low for crops at a critical stage, such as flowering. It can happen even with near-normal rain if the rain arrives at the wrong time. 3. **Hydrological drought:** rivers, lakes, reservoirs and groundwater fall low. It lags behind the rain deficit by months or years. 4. **Socio-economic drought:** demand for water outstrips supply, causing crop failure, livestock deaths, migration and sometimes famine.

What causes drought#

  • Ocean–atmosphere cycles: El Niño weakens the Indian and Southeast Asian monsoons and dries southern Africa and Australia; La Niña dries the Horn of Africa and the southwestern USA.
  • Blocking high-pressure systems that park over a region for weeks.
  • Land feedbacks: dry soil heats up and reduces local rain, deepening the drought.
  • Human factors: groundwater overuse, deforestation, thirsty crops in dry places, and warming, which increases evaporation (so-called “hot droughts”).

Droughts that changed history#

Drought When What happened
4.2-kiloyear event ~2200 BCE A centuries-long dry spell linked to the decline of Egypt’s Old Kingdom, the Akkadian Empire and possibly the mature Harappan cities
Great Famine (India) 1876–78 A strong El Niño failed the monsoon; 5.5–10 million died under colonial grain-export policies
Dust Bowl (USA) 1930s Ploughed-up prairie blew away, displacing millions and creating the US Soil Conservation Service
Sahel droughts 1968–1985 Decades of low rain across West Africa; ~100,000+ deaths and mass migration
Maharashtra 1972 Led to India’s Employment Guarantee Scheme, the forerunner of MGNREGA
Millennium Drought (Australia) 1997–2009 Rewrote water law in the Murray–Darling basin
Syria 2006–2010 The worst drought in the instrumental record, made 2–3× more likely by warming (Kelley et al. 2015)
Horn of Africa 2020–23 Five failed rainy seasons in a row, the longest on record there
Southern Africa 2024 El Niño drought destroyed maize harvests across Zambia, Zimbabwe and Malawi
Amazon 2023–24 Rio Negro at Manaus fell to its lowest level since 1902

How droughts are measured#

  • SPI (Standardised Precipitation Index) compares rain with the long-term record.
  • SPEI adds evaporation, so it captures heat-driven droughts.
  • Soil moisture from satellites such as SMAP and SMOS.
  • Vegetation health (NDVI) from MODIS and Sentinel.
  • GRACE satellites weigh groundwater changes from orbit; they revealed the rapid depletion of northwest India’s aquifers.

Coping with drought#

Traditional systems such as stepwells, johads, kuls and tank cascades; watershed development (Ralegan Siddhi, Hiware Bazar); drought-tolerant crops (millets, sorghum, pulses); drip irrigation; crop insurance; early-warning systems; and employment guarantees that keep money flowing in bad years.

Sources: UNCCD: Drought · Kelley et al. 2015, PNAS · Wikipedia: Drought
