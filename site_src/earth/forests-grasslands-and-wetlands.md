A **biome** is a large region defined by its climate and the vegetation it supports. Land biomes range from dense rainforest to open grassland and wetland, and each supports its own soils, animals and kinds of farming.

## Forests

Forests cover about **4.06 billion hectares**, roughly 31% of land (FAO Global Forest Resources Assessment 2020).

:::collapse !The main forest biomes
- **Tropical rainforest** (Amazon, Congo, Borneo, Western Ghats, Northeast India): over 2,000 mm of rain a year, no cold season, the richest biodiversity on land. Nutrients are locked in the trees, not the soil, so cleared rainforest land often loses fertility within a few years.
- **Tropical dry and monsoon forest** (central India, Southeast Asia): trees such as **teak** and **sal** shed leaves in the dry season.
- **Temperate deciduous forest** (Europe, eastern USA, eastern China): oak, beech and maple on fertile brown soils; much of it was cleared for farming centuries ago.
- **Temperate rainforest** (Pacific Northwest, southern Chile, New Zealand).
- **Boreal forest (taiga):** the largest land biome, across Russia, Scandinavia and Canada; conifers on acidic podzols; a huge carbon store now hit by wildfires (Canada lost about 18 million hectares to fire in 2023).
- **Mediterranean woodland and scrub** (maquis, chaparral, fynbos): fire-adapted shrubs, olives and cork oak.
:::

### Deforestation, then and now

The world lost about 420 million hectares of forest between 1990 and 2020, mostly converted to farmland and pasture. Hope comes from the Amazon: Brazil's official PRODES system measured **3,335 km²** cleared in August 2025–July 2026, a 41.8% drop and the lowest since monitoring began in 1988, while clearing in the **Cerrado** fell about 24%.

## Grasslands

Grasslands cover about a quarter to two-fifths of land depending on definition and include:
- **Tropical savanna**: grasses with scattered trees (Serengeti, Sahel margins, Deccan scrub, northern Australia), shaped by fire and grazing.
- **Temperate grassland**: the **Eurasian steppe**, **North American prairie**, **Argentine pampas** and **South African veld**. Their deep, dark **mollisol** and **chernozem** soils make them the world's breadbaskets, which is why most have been ploughed.
- **Montane grassland**: alpine meadows and Indian *shola*-grassland mosaics in the Nilgiris.

India's grasslands, such as the **Banni** in Kutch and the **Terai** grasslands, are often wrongly classed as "wasteland" and planted with trees; they are home to the great Indian bustard, blackbuck and wild ass.

## Wetlands

Wetlands cover only about 6% of land but store a third of soil carbon and filter water, buffer floods and nurture fisheries. About 35% of wetlands have been lost since 1970.

:::facts Great wetlands
- **Pantanal** (Brazil, Bolivia, Paraguay): the largest tropical wetland
- **Sundarbans** (India, Bangladesh): the largest mangrove forest, home of the Bengal tiger
- **Okavango Delta** (Botswana): a river that floods the Kalahari
- **The Sudd** (South Sudan): a vast Nile swamp
- **Peatlands** of Indonesia, the Congo Basin and Siberia: waterlogged soils packed with carbon
- India's **Ramsar sites** include Chilika Lake, Keoladeo, Loktak and the Sundarbans
:::
