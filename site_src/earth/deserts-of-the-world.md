A **desert** is land that receives less than about **250 mm of precipitation a year**, or where evaporation far exceeds rainfall. By that definition about a third of Earth's land is desert or semi-desert, and the largest desert of all is not the Sahara but **Antarctica**.

## Why deserts form

:::collapse !Five causes of dryness
1. **Subtropical high pressure.** Air rising at the equator descends around 20–30° north and south, warming and drying as it sinks. This belt holds the Sahara, Arabian, Thar, Kalahari and Australian deserts.
2. **Rain shadows.** Mountains strip moisture from wind: the Gobi and Taklamakan lie behind the Himalaya and Tibetan Plateau; Patagonia lies behind the Andes.
3. **Distance from the sea (continentality).** Air crossing thousands of kilometres of land loses moisture; Central Asia's Kyzylkum and Karakum are far from any ocean.
4. **Cold ocean currents.** Cold water cools the air above it so it cannot rise and rain. The Humboldt Current feeds the Atacama; the Benguela Current feeds the Namib. These coastal deserts are often foggy but almost rainless.
5. **Polar cold.** Freezing air holds little moisture, so Antarctica and the high Arctic are deserts.
:::

## Kinds of desert

| Kind | Examples | Features |
|---|---|---|
| Hot subtropical | Sahara, Arabian, Thar, Great Victoria, Mojave | Summer days above 45 °C; cold nights; sand seas (ergs) and rocky plains (regs, hamadas) |
| Cold (mid-latitude) | Gobi, Taklamakan, Ladakh, Patagonian | Freezing winters, hot summers |
| Coastal fog | Atacama, Namib | Cool, foggy, almost no rain |
| Polar | Antarctica, Arctic | Ice-covered or bare frozen ground |
| Salt deserts and flats | Rann of Kutch, Salar de Uyuni, Dasht-e Kavir | Evaporated lakes and seasonal floods |

:::facts Desert records
- Largest hot desert: **Sahara**, ~9.2 million km², about the size of the USA
- Driest non-polar place: **Atacama**, where some stations average under 1 mm a year
- Oldest desert: **Namib**, dry for ~55–80 million years
- Highest air temperature recorded: 56.7 °C, **Death Valley**, 1913 (disputed by some climatologists)
- Highest land-surface temperatures: ~70 °C+ measured by satellite in Iran's **Lut Desert**
- Most populous desert: the **Thar**, with roughly 80+ people per km² in places
:::

## The Thar and India's dry west

The **Thar Desert** covers about 200,000 km² across western Rajasthan, parts of Gujarat, Haryana and Punjab, and Pakistan's Sindh. Rainfall falls from about 500 mm in the east to under 100 mm near Jaisalmer, almost all in July–September. Communities adapted with stepwells (*baoris*), village ponds (*johads*), underground tanks (*kundis/tankas*) and drought-hardy crops: **pearl millet (bajra)**, cluster bean (**guar**), moth bean and the desert tree **khejri** (*Prosopis cineraria*). The **Indira Gandhi Canal** now irrigates parts of the desert, turning sand into wheat and mustard fields but also causing waterlogging and salinity.

## Life in deserts

Desert plants store water (cacti, euphorbias), grow deep roots (acacias, khejri), or live as seeds waiting years for rain. Animals avoid daytime heat, get water from food and concentrate their urine; the dromedary camel can lose up to a quarter of its body water and recover. Fog-basking beetles in the Namib drink droplets that condense on their backs.

## Deserts change

- The **Sahara was green** about 11,000–5,000 years ago during the African Humid Period, with lakes, hippos and cattle herders, because a slight wobble in Earth's orbit strengthened the monsoon. Rock art of swimmers survives in Egypt's Gilf Kebir.
- Today, **desertification** (land degradation in drylands, mainly from overgrazing, poor farming and drought) threatens drylands home to about two billion people. Up to 40% of the world's land is degraded, according to the UN. See [land degradation](/wiki/soil/land-degradation-and-desertification.html).
- Restoration works: Niger's farmers regrew trees on ~5 million hectares by protecting stumps, and China's Loess Plateau project restored eroded hills.
