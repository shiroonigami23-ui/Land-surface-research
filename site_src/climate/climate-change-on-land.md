title: Climate Change on Land
summary: Land is warming faster than the global average. What that means for heat, rain, ice, forests, soils and crops, and how land can help fix the problem.
level: Intermediate
---
Since pre-industrial times, the **land surface has warmed about 1.6 °C**, considerably more than the global average (about 1.1–1.3 °C when oceans are included), because land has less water to evaporate and smaller heat capacity (IPCC Special Report on Climate Change and Land, 2019).

## What is changing

:::collapse !Seven big shifts
1. **Heat:** extreme heat is more frequent and intense. Heatwaves over 45 °C in northern India and Pakistan, and over 50 °C in the Middle East, now occur more often.
2. **Rain:** the water cycle intensifies. Wet extremes get wetter and dry spells longer; dry subtropics expand.
3. **Ice:** mountain glaciers lost 408 Gt in 2025 alone, and every glacier region has shrunk four years in a row.
4. **Permafrost** is thawing, releasing carbon.
5. **Fire:** fire seasons are longer, from Canada's record 2023 season to Australia's Black Summer.
6. **Seasons:** springs start earlier; crops and wild plants flower sooner; pests and diseases move to higher latitudes and altitudes.
7. **Soils:** warmer soils lose carbon faster, and heavier rain erodes them.
:::

## Crops and food

Every 1 °C of global warming is estimated to cut global yields of **maize by ~7.4%**, **wheat by ~6.0%**, **rice by ~3.2%** and **soybean by ~3.1%** without adaptation (Zhao et al. 2017, PNAS). Wheat in north India is especially sensitive to heat in February–March at grain filling, as the March 2022 heatwave showed.

## Land is also part of the solution

Land absorbs roughly **a third of the CO₂** humans emit each year, mostly in forests and soils, while agriculture, forestry and land use cause about **22% of greenhouse emissions** (IPCC). Options include:
- **Stopping deforestation**, as in the Amazon's sharp decline in 2025–26;
- **Restoring degraded land and forests** (the Bonn Challenge, Great Green Wall);
- **Building soil carbon** through cover crops, reduced tillage, agroforestry and biochar;
- **Protecting peatlands and wetlands**;
- **Cutting food waste and shifting diets**, which frees land.
