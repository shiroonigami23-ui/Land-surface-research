Fruits are the seed-bearing parts of flowering plants, and the ones we eat have mostly been bred over thousands of years from wild ancestors scattered across the world. Where a fruit grows depends above all on **winter cold**: tropical fruits die with frost, while temperate fruits need a winter **chilling period** to flower properly.

## Four fruit belts

:::collapse !From the equator to the cool temperate zone
- **Tropical** (no frost, warm all year): mango, banana, papaya, pineapple, jackfruit, coconut, durian, mangosteen, guava. Grown across Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Southeast Asia, the Caribbean, Central and South America and Africa.
- **Subtropical** (mild winters, occasional light frost): citrus (orange, lemon, lime), lychee, pomegranate, avocado, guava. Nagpur (oranges), Bihar (lychee), Maharashtra (pomegranate), Brazil, Spain, Florida.
- **Mediterranean** (hot dry summers, wet mild winters): grapes, olives, figs, almonds, dates (in the hot desert margins), apricots.
- **Temperate** (cold winters, needing roughly 800–1,500 chilling hours below 7 °C for many apple varieties): apples, pears, cherries, plums, peaches, walnuts, strawberries, blueberries. Kashmir, Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand, China, Europe and North America.
:::

## Where favourite fruits came from

- **Mango:** northeastern India and Myanmar; cultivated in India for over 4,000 years.
- **Banana:** New Guinea and Southeast Asia; the seedless edible banana is a hybrid of *Musa acuminata* and *M. balbisiana*.
- **Apple:** the wild apple forests of the **Tian Shan** mountains of Kazakhstan; spread along the Silk Road.
- **Citrus:** the foothills of the eastern Himalaya, Assam, Myanmar and southern China.
- **Grapes:** the Caucasus, where wine-making goes back 8,000 years (Georgia).
- **Avocado and cacao:** Mesoamerica and the upper Amazon.

## India and fruit

India is the **world's second-largest fruit producer** after China and the largest producer of **mangoes, bananas, papayas and guavas**. Notable regional fruits: Alphonso (Ratnagiri), Kesar (Gujarat) and Dasheri (Malihabad) mangoes; Nagpur oranges; Muzaffarpur shahi lychee; Kashmiri and Himachal apples; Nashik grapes; Solapur pomegranates; Jalgaon bananas; and the *jamun*, *amla*, *ber*, *bael* and *karonda* of Indian forests and drylands, often hardier and more nutritious than exotics.
